Coordinates are instructions for combining basis vectors

A coordinate pair tells us how much of each basis vector to take. Watch 3 copies of the horizontal basis vector and 2 copies of the vertical basis vector combine to make (3, 2).

Blue = horizontal basis contribution. Orange = vertical basis contribution. Purple = final vector. The dashed lines show the horizontal and vertical movement.

Step 1 · The standard basis

Start with two basis vectors

î = 10 ĵ = 01
Our coordinates
32 = 3î + 2ĵ
The standard basis vectors are one unit to the right and one unit up.
3î = (3, 0)
2ĵ = (0, 2)
Result = (3, 2)

Remember: The numbers 3 and 2 are not just a location label. They tell us how much to scale each chosen basis vector before adding them.

What does “relative to a basis” mean?

The pair (3, 2) describes the vector using the standard basis î and ĵ. If we choose a different basis, the same geometric vector can have different coordinates. The vector itself has not changed—only the instructions used to describe it have changed.
