Why a 90° rotation has no real eigenvectors

Eigenvector test
For an eigenvector, Rv = λv. The rotated vector must stay on the original line. Here, it turns onto a perpendicular line instead.

Connect the picture to the calculation

The geometry shows that no non-zero real vector stays on its original line. The characteristic equation confirms this algebraically.

R − λI = −λ−11−λ

det(R − λI) = (−λ)(−λ) − (−1)(1) = λ² + 1

λ² + 1 = 0 ⟹ λ² = −1 ⟹ λ = ±i

Why no real eigenvalues? No real number squared gives −1. The solutions +i and −i are complex numbers, not real numbers. Therefore, this rotation has no real eigenvalues and no real eigenvectors.
