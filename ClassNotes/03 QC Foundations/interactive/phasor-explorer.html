Phase: an angle. Phasor: an arrow.

Imagine an arrow turning around a circle like a clock hand. Its angle tells you the phase. Its length tells you the amplitude (size). This arrow is called a phasor.

What does eiφ mean?

eiφ = cos φ + i sin φ

This is a compact way to describe the tip of a length-1 arrow. You do not need to calculate the exponential to use the picture.

Horizontal part: cos φ

How far right or left the tip is. This is called the real part.

Vertical part: sin φ

How far up or down the tip is. This is called the imaginary part. Here “imaginary” is the mathematical name for the vertical coordinate.

For example, at 90° the arrow points straight up. Its tip is (0, 1), so eiπ/2 = 0 + i × 1 = i.

A general phasor is Aeiφ: A controls its length and φ controls its angle. This explorer keeps A = 1 to focus on phase.

Why does an arrow describe a wave?

As the arrow turns at a steady speed, its tip goes up and down smoothly. Plot that vertical height against time, and you get a sine wave.

Arrow: ei(ωt + φ)   →   vertical height: sin(ωt + φ)

t means time. ω (omega) tells you how fast the arrow turns. φ (phi) is its angle at time 0. A full turn is 360° or 2π radians.

“Play one cycle” sweeps the starting phase through one full turn. It shows a new starting arrow and shifted wave at each angle. For one fixed wave, following its height forward in time gives the same circular motion.

This uses the vertical projection (sine). Choosing the horizontal projection gives a cosine wave. Both are valid conventions; they differ by a quarter cycle.

Global phase and relative phase

For two arrows, the relative phase is the angle between them. Turn both by the same amount and that angle stays unchanged. Turn only one and the angle changes.

Phase differenceTwo equal waves at the same location
0°Peaks line up: they reinforce.
180°Peak meets trough: they cancel.

In quantum mechanics, multiplying the entire state by eiφ changes its global phase and does not change measurement predictions. Changing the phase of one component relative to another can change interference.

For ordinary waves, phase can be measured relative to an external clock or reference wave. The statement “global phase is unobservable” applies exactly to the overall phase of a quantum state.
