Two speakers: shared delay versus extra delay

Both speakers receive the same steady tone. A delay moves its peaks later in time. Compare a delay shared by both speakers with an extra delay in only one branch.

In the professor’s original chain, if the first and second boxes add delays Δ₁ and Δ₂, the cumulative time after both is t₀ + Δ₁ + Δ₂. If Δ₂ instead names the total delay from the source, t₀ + Δ₂ is correct. Here Δ always means the total shared delay.

The boxes are labelled delay because we are studying timing. An amplifier changes signal size; its delay must be specified separately. This example keeps both waves equally strong, with equal travel times to the listener.

Degrees describe fractions of a cycle: 90° = ¼ cycle; 180° = ½ cycle; 360° = one full cycle. A positive delay produces a negative phase shift.

A phasor’s angle represents phase; its length represents amplitude. Each speaker’s arrow has length 1.

A(t) = sin(ωt − ωΔ)
B(t) = sin(ωt − ωΔ − ωδ)

ω is the angular frequency: how fast the phase turns. The graph uses a steady tone extending through time, rather than a sound turning on at t₀. Cancellation assumes equal waves arriving at this particular listener.

3. What the professor means

Shared shift: both delayed equally

The whole pair moves later. Their separation stays the same, so the interference strength stays the same. This illustrates a common phase shift.

If you listen without an external timing reference, the same steady tone sounds the same. Against a clock, the delay can be detected.

Relative shift: only one delayed

The peaks no longer occur together. Now the waves can reinforce less, or cancel. The phase difference between A and B controls this interference.

A delay does not make a steady tone into a different note. Its frequency (pitch) stays the same; its timing changes.

Quantum connection: an overall factor eiφ multiplying the entire quantum state is an unobservable global phase. Changing the phase of one component relative to another can affect interference after suitable operations. The speaker example explains the relationship between waves, but a common sound delay can still be measured against an external reference.

Phasor is the correct word here, rather than “phaser.” For a length-1 arrow, eiφ = cos φ + i sin φ. Its horizontal coordinate is cos φ; its vertical coordinate is sin φ.
