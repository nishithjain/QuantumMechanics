T

The \(T\) gate is a one-qubit gate that adds a \(45^\circ\) phase to the \(\lvert1\rangle\) part of a qubit state. It leaves the \(\lvert0\rangle\) part alone.

\[T\lvert0\rangle = \lvert0\rangle,\qquad T\lvert1\rangle = e^{i\pi/4}\lvert1\rangle.\]

Think of \(e^{i\pi/4}\) as an instruction to rotate the complex amplitude by \(45^\circ\) while keeping its length the same.

For example, apply \(T\) to the familiar state \(\lvert+\rangle\):

\[\underbrace{\frac{\lvert0\rangle+\lvert1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}}_{\lvert+\rangle} \quad\xrightarrow{\ T\ }\quad \frac{\lvert0\rangle+e^{i\pi/4}\lvert1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}.\]

If you measure either state directly in the \(Z\) basis, you still get 50% chance of \(0\) and 50% chance of \(1\). The difference is the relative phase between the two parts. A later gate such as \(H\) can turn that phase difference into a change in measurement probabilities.
