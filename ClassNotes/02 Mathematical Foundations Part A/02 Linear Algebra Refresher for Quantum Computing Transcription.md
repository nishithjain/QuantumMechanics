- span{v₁, …, vₖ} = {α₁v₁ + ··· + αₖvₖ : αⱼ ∈ ℂ}.
- Linear independence: α₁v₁ + ··· + αₖvₖ = 0 implies every αⱼ = 0.
- A basis is a linearly independent set that spans the entire space. In ℂⁿ, every basis has n vectors.
- Standard basis of ℂ²: e₁ = (1, 0)ᵀ and e₂ = (0, 1)ᵀ.
- Hadamard basis of ℂ²: h₊ = (1, 1)ᵀ/√2, h₋ = (1, −1)ᵀ/√2.
- For v = (a, b)ᵀ, coordinates in the Hadamard basis are ((a+b)/√2, (a−b)/√2).

**Complex inner product and norm (slides 25–40):**

- v† = (conjugate(v₁), …, conjugate(vₙ)) is the conjugate transpose.
- ⟨u, v⟩ = u†v = Σⱼ conjugate(uⱼ)vⱼ.
- ⟨v, v⟩ = Σⱼ |vⱼ|² ≥ 0; ||v|| = √⟨v, v⟩.
- ⟨v, u⟩ = conjugate(⟨u, v⟩).
- ⟨αu, v⟩ = conjugate(α)⟨u, v⟩ and ⟨u, αv⟩ = α⟨u, v⟩.
- A nonzero vector is normalised by v̂ = v / ||v||.

**Orthogonality and orthonormal bases (slides 41–53):**

- Cauchy–Schwarz: |⟨u, v⟩| ≤ ||u|| ||v||.
- Triangle inequality: ||u+v|| ≤ ||u|| + ||v||.
- u ⟂ v precisely when ⟨u, v⟩ = 0.
- An orthonormal basis {eⱼ} has ⟨eᵢ, eⱼ⟩ = δᵢⱼ.
- Expansion in an orthonormal basis: v = Σⱼ ⟨eⱼ, v⟩eⱼ.
- The slides' worked example (pp. 51–53): if v = (3/5, 4i/5)ᵀ, then ⟨h₊, v⟩ = (3+4i)/(5√2) and ⟨h₋, v⟩ = (3−4i)/(5√2).
