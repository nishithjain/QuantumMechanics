The line y = 1 is not a subspace

Vectors on the line fail closure under addition; scaling by 0 sends you to the origin, which is not on the line.

Subspace counterexample

What is a subspace?

A set W inside a vector space is a subspace if:

  1. Closed under addition: if u, w ∈ W, then u + w ∈ W.
  2. Closed under scalar multiplication: if u ∈ W and α ∈ ℝ, then αu ∈ W.

Check W = {(x, 1) | x ∈ ℝ}

Rule 1 (addition) fails: u = (2, 1) and w = (−1, 1) lie on y = 1, but u + w = (1, 2) does not.

Rule 2 (scaling) fails: 0·u = (0, 0) is the origin, which is not on y = 1.

Conclusion: The line y = 1 is not a subspace.
